Low-Cost Telescope (LCT) Project

Democratizing observational astronomy training through affordable hardware, authentic data acquisition pipelines, and scalability for K-12 and undergraduate education.

Project Lead: Inseok Song • University of Georgia

Project Overview

The Low-Cost Telescope (LCT) Project addresses a critical gap in astronomy education: the lack of scalable, hands-on observational experiences. Traditional astronomy labs heavily rely on expensive, department-owned telescopes, severely limiting individual student access and reducing opportunities for student-driven learning.

Our solution provides a model where each student has access to an individually operable, low-cost telescope setup paired with accessible digital imaging detectors (like smartphones or low-cost CMOS modules). This methodology shifts the paradigm from remote data consumption to authentic, end-to-end scientific processes—including instrument physical setup, target alignment, calibration, and data reduction.

Why LCT? Core Advantages

  • Hands-on Instrumentation: Students learn telescope setup, optical collimation, system alignment, and sensor calibration natively.
  • Authentic Data Pipeline: End-to-end processing pipeline—from photon capture to dark/flat frames subtraction, frame stacking, and error analysis.
  • High Access, High Skills: Moving closer to a 1:1 student-to-telescope model enables repeated trial, flexible scheduling, and deeper analytical proficiency.
  • Scalable Deployment: Multi-tiered configuration options adapt perfectly to K-12 budgets, general undergraduate courses, and advanced astrophysics labs.

Implementation Workflow

1. Configuration Selection

Select Tier B1–B4 depending on course constraints, student prerequisite technical levels, and regional sky-brightness criteria.

2. Assembly & Optical Alignment

Students assemble mechanical fixtures, establish critical optical alignment (collimation), and initialize detector focus limits.

3. Target Acquisition & Data Collection

Execution of scheduled observation sessions. Students capture light frames alongside bias, dark, and flat frames for mandatory image calibration.

4. Data Processing & Stacking

Calibrating images using raw darks/flats, aligning frame coordinates, and stacking signals to optimize Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR).

5. Analysis & Quantitative Reporting

Conducting final analysis (e.g., differential aperture photometry, astrometric tracking) and compiling results in standard lab note formats.
